# The World

The world is shaped by the tension between two forces: Hope and Fear.

Above the mortal realm lie the Hallows Above — the domain of the New Gods, beings of immense power who watch over the world and grant their blessings to the faithful. Temples stand in their honor, and their seraphs walk among mortals as divine champions.

Below lies a darker place — the Circles Below, where the Fallen Gods were cast down long ago, defeated and imprisoned by the New Gods in a war that reshaped creation itself. The Fallen are not gone. They scheme in their prisons, and their whispers still reach the mortal world through cults, artifacts, and forgotten places. Their followers seek to restore what was lost — or to tear down what replaced it.

Between these two realms lies the Mortal Realm — a vast and varied world of many peoples. Humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, faeries, firbolgs, fungi, goblins, giants, katari, ribbet, simiah, and many others share this land. Some live in great cities, others in quiet villages, still others in the wilds between. Magic is real and present — woven into the fabric of the world, studied in academies, channeled through instinct, or granted by the divine.

It is a world where heroes have risen before. And where they will be needed again.

# The Great War

A thousand years ago, a great evil rose in this part of the world. The stories disagree on what it was — a demon lord, a fallen angel, a god's wayward servant. Some songs describe a figure of terrible beauty. Others speak of darkness and fire. The details have worn smooth with retelling, like a river stone.

What everyone agrees on is that heroes rose to meet it. A band of champions — their names still sung in taverns, though the verses vary from town to town — fought a long and costly war, and in the end, they won. The evil was defeated. The land was saved. The heroes were honored, and life went on.

That was a thousand years ago. Long enough for the war to become a legend, and the legend to become a bedtime story. Long enough that no one remembers what was actually fought, or why, or what it would look like if it ever came back.
